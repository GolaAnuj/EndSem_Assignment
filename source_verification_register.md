# Source Verification Register - Prompt 02

**Purpose:** Record what has been checked against primary-source pages and what remains to be checked. This is not an exhaustive systematic review.

## VERIFIED at primary-source abstract/page level

### Putnam and Phelps (2017) - The citation effect
- **Checked:** PubMed/ScienceDirect abstract for six experiments and reported `d = 0.13`, 95% CI `[0.06, 0.20]`.
- **Why it matters:** Supports the review's distinction between citation presence and specific formatting effects.
- **Still needed:** Inspect the full article for the design details most relevant to adapting the stimuli.
- **Link:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28780441/

### Walters and Wilder (2023) - Fabrication and errors in the bibliographic citations generated by ChatGPT
- **Checked:** Scientific Reports/PubMed abstract: 42 topics, 84 short papers, 636 references overall; GPT-3.5/GPT-4 fabrication and error proportions.
- **Why it matters:** Supports the existence of fabricated/inaccurate references in the studied LLM output.
- **Still needed:** Check table denominators, definitions, and supplement-level data before creating any stimulus corpus.
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-023-41032-5

### Bhattacharyya et al. (2023) - High rates of fabricated and inaccurate references in ChatGPT-generated medical content
- **Checked:** PubMed abstract: 115 references; 47% fabricated, 46% authentic but inaccurate, 7% authentic and accurate.
- **Why it matters:** Supports generation-accuracy concerns; it does not itself measure how much people trust a citation.
- **Still needed:** Check stimulus details, field-level errors, and any available supplementary data.
- **Link:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37337480/

### Kim et al. (2025) - Fostering appropriate reliance on large language models
- **Checked:** Author/institution publication pages and abstract: preregistered controlled experiment (`N = 308`); source and explanation factors; sources lowered reliance on incorrect responses.
- **Why it matters:** Adjacent work on source presentation and user reliance.
- **Still needed:** Read full methods and results before adapting it to citation-format conditions.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08554

### Wineburg and McGrew (2019) - Lateral reading and the nature of expertise
- **Checked:** Journal abstract/page: 45 participants across professional fact-checkers, historians, and undergraduates; source-evaluation strategies were compared.
- **Why it matters:** Supports lateral reading as a general online source-evaluation strategy, not proof of a citation-specific intervention effect.
- **Still needed:** Keep that distinction explicit if GAP 04 is selected.
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1177/016146811912101102

### Ding et al. (2025) - Citations and Trust in LLM Generated Responses
- **Checked:** Official AAAI abstract: a live question-answering experiment varied zero/one/five citations and relevant/random citations; the study recorded citation checking and self-reported trust. Its abstract reports increased trust when citations were present, including random ones.
- **Why it matters:** Directly qualifies broad claims that citation presence/count/relevance or checking behavior have never been studied.
- **Still needed:** Compare the study's actual factors with the proposed metadata-completeness and DOI/PMID conditions.
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v39i22.34550

## Leads located; full-text/methods not yet analyzed

### Cho and Lim (2026) - How Source Attribution Visualization Shapes User Attention and Preference
- **Located:** Bibliographic record and abstract for an eye-tracking study of four AI-chatbot source-attribution layouts.
- **Why it matters:** Potential overlap with visual/source-display formatting.
- **Next check:** Inspect the exact manipulated visual features, outcomes, stimuli, and conclusions before using it to describe a remaining gap.
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.3390/jemr19040089

### Han et al. (2026) - Phantom citations: An empirical study of non-existent and unverifiable references in scholarly literature
- **Located:** Journal record and abstract about developing an automated/auditable method for detecting non-existent or unverifiable references.
- **Why it matters:** Adjacent to an automated-verification fallback, not direct evidence that the human format-trust question has been answered.
- **Next check:** Inspect its methods/data access and distinguish automated detection from human trust measures.
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1002/asi.70104

## Remaining checks before freezing a research question

1. Read full methods, stimuli, and measures for Ding et al. (2025), Kim et al. (2025), and Cho and Lim (2026); compare their independent variables with GAP 01 and GAP 02.
2. Inspect tables and supplementary files from Walters and Wilder (2023) and Bhattacharyya et al. (2023) before extracting any study stimulus corpus.
3. Conduct a documented multi-database search using terms such as `citation completeness`, `reference formatting`, `DOI presence`, `PMID cue`, `fabricated citation`, `citation credibility`, `citation trust`, `LLM sources`, and `citation checking behavior`.
4. If making an originality claim later, record search date, databases, exact queries, inclusion/exclusion rules, and screened results.

**Novelty status: UNKNOWN.** The targeted search reduces the chance of an overbroad claim but is not systematic and does not establish that any gap is new.
